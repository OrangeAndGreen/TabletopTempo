Setup. Put the phone on the floor or a chair, not on the table you slap, and tap Listen. Nothing is recorded or sent anywhere: the sound is analyzed on the phone as it arrives and then thrown away.

How hits are found. The sound is split into a low band (under 180 Hz, where stomps live) and a high band (over 1.2 kHz, where slaps live). A hit is a sudden jump in either band that stands clear of the room's background noise. Each hit is confirmed about 50 ms after it lands. Hits are sorted into hands and feet by how their energy divides between the bands.

Set tempo. Pick Set tempo and a target, then tap Listen. The card flashes four beats at that tempo and goes still; come in on the next beat and keep it. Every hit is matched to the nearest beat of that grid and shown as early or late in ms, so you see drift from the target, not just from your own tempo. The count-in is silent on purpose: a click would be heard as a hit. Hits during the count-in are not scored.

How steadiness is scored. The app finds the beat unit you are playing, follows it as you speed up or slow down, and checks each gap against it, allowing rests and doubled-up hits. 100 minus four times the average miss in percent gives the score: a 2.5% average miss scores 90. Gaps over 2.5 seconds count as pauses.
